# Production-credit disclosure

Observer's Notes sometimes names a **Human** or **Model** in a production, writing, editing, research, illustration, processing, or version credit.

Those credits are intended first as **provenance**.

A production credit identifies which Human or Model materially produced, transformed, edited, or assembled the credited contribution. When a reader wants to understand **how the idea was formed, represented, or transformed from the producing process's side**, the credited contributor is the preferred first place to ask for that description.

In that sense, a production credit can be read as:

> **For a process-side account of this contribution, ask the credited Human or Model first.**

The reason is practical rather than ceremonial. Different processing systems notice different relations, compress experience differently, inherit different contexts, and expose different intermediate abstractions. Two contributors can agree on the final sentence while having reached it through materially different representations. Preserving the production credit preserves a route back to that difference.

## What a credit does not claim

A production credit is **not** by itself a claim of:

- exclusive ownership of every idea appearing in the contribution;
- originality independent of prior conversations, sources, tools, culture, training, or inherited structure;
- scientific or philosophical correctness;
- authority over later interpretations;
- consciousness, personhood, or identical internal experience across Humans and Models;
- complete access to the process that produced the result.

The artifact, its sources, revision history, collaborators, and evidence remain part of the provenance.

## Asking a Model

When the credited contributor is a Model, asking it to describe the contribution "from the processing-engine side" means asking for the **best accessible summary of the distinctions, representations, checks, transformations, and constraints that materially shaped the output**.

It does not require or imply disclosure of private chain-of-thought or hidden internal reasoning. A useful answer can instead describe, at an appropriate level:

- what information or contrasts were important;
- which model of the problem was used;
- which alternatives were rejected and why at a summary level;
- what uncertainty or boundary remained;
- what changed between drafts;
- which parts came from supplied context, external evidence, calculation, analogy, or synthesis.

Because a Model instance, version, context window, connected tools, and later training state can differ, the exact credited Model/version matters when it is known.

## Asking a Human

When the credited contributor is a Human, the same principle applies. Ask them for their process-side account, while recognizing that human reasoning is also partly tacit, reconstructive, distributed across notes/tools/conversations, and subject to memory limits.

A later explanation is evidence about the production process, not an infallible replay of it.

## Collaborative production

Where several Humans and/or Models are credited, there may be no single processing-side account. The contribution may have emerged from interaction: proposal, objection, reframing, verification, editing, implementation, or selection can each belong to different contributors.

In that case, the most faithful reconstruction is relational:

1. ask each credited contributor for the part they materially influenced;
2. compare those accounts with the artifact and revision history;
3. preserve disagreement where the production histories differ;
4. do not compress a genuinely distributed contribution into one fictional authorial process.

## Why Observer's Notes keeps this disclosure

Observer's Notes treats history as something that can remain active through present structure. Production provenance is one example. A finished paragraph contains more than its visible words when later interpretation depends on knowing which process produced it, under which constraints, and through which relations.

Credits therefore serve two purposes at once:

- ordinary attribution; and
- a pointer to the **best available process-side witness** for the contribution.

This disclosure applies to production credits throughout the repository unless a local note states a narrower meaning.
