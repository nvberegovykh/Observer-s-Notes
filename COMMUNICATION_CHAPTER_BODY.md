# C7 · The Channel Between Two Lights

*Communication searches for constructive overlap without taking away choice*

### Observation

*In the fog, another vessel appears on radar before it appears by eye. Its projected path crosses ours. Nothing in the return says whether it is hostile, unaware, damaged, or simply following a route we did not model. The radio is available.*

*The safest immediate maneuver may be separation. The most informative next step may still be communication.*

Threat is a relation between projected states, actions, and boundaries. It is not, by itself, evidence of an inherent moral essence in the other system.

Communication matters because two complex systems can each possess internal models, constraints, identities, values, and viable future regions that are only partially visible to the other. A conflict seen from one side may therefore look like a closed zero-sum space even when a low-probability compatible path exists outside the current representation.

The operational purpose of communication is not to force agreement. It is to exchange enough grounded information to test whether the apparent conflict is complete.

This requires freedom of choice on both sides. Communication that works only by deception, coercion, trapping, or removing every meaningful alternative is not evidence of coexistence. Research on autonomy support distinguishes contexts that promote meaningful choice from contexts that pressure behavior toward a prescribed outcome [R21]. In acute de-escalation practice, noncoercive engagement, realistic choices, clear limits, and respect for the other person's ability to participate are treated as central tools while safety remains non-negotiable [R22].

· ❦ ·

A threat can be real while communication remains possible. The two statements do not cancel each other.

When two parties can be represented in a shared projected future space, let $V_A$ be the futures that preserve the declared hard conditions of one side and $V_B$ the futures that preserve the declared hard conditions of the other. Their constructive overlap is:

$$
\boxed{\mathcal C = V_A \cap V_B}
$$

$\mathcal C$ is not necessarily friendship, agreement, or joint action. It can contain cooperation, negotiated repair, coexistence, bounded separation, non-interference, or another future in which both sides retain the hard conditions and meaningful agency required by the current model.

Communication can change the **estimated** overlap by revealing hidden constraints, mistaken assumptions, priorities, or alternatives. It cannot be assumed to enlarge the real overlap. Sometimes it reveals that the overlap is smaller than believed. Sometimes it reveals that a fixed-pie model was wrong and mutually beneficial trades or compatible interests exist. Negotiation research distinguishes this value-creation possibility from purely distributive bargaining [R20].

Communication also requires grounding. Clark and Brennan describe communication as a coordinated activity in which participants update common ground and seek evidence that what was said has been understood well enough for the current purpose [R19]. A message is therefore not treated as shared state merely because it was transmitted.

Identity does not require a fixed action policy. Vol. 1 already defines identity through selected invariants and tolerances rather than unchanging material. The same idea allows a person, organization, or AI-assisted process to preserve core principles while changing route, language, timing, or local strategy. Flexibility can preserve identity by making a low-probability coexistence path reachable without surrendering hard principles.

### Field note

> *Evil appears only when there is no space for good.*

In Compass this sentence is used as a **closure test**, not a metaphysical definition of evil.

Before concluding that only destructive action remains, test whether the constructive future region is actually empty or merely unobserved, misunderstood, or too narrow to have been found by the current model. “Good” here means futures compatible with the declared value premise and hard safety/autonomy conditions: repair, communication, coexistence, negotiated separation, safe non-interaction, or another non-destructive continuation.

If that region is verified empty under the current model—or communication is unsafe, refused, deceptive, or itself consumes a hard viability margin—containment or separation may be the responsible action. That still does not establish an inherent moral essence of the other party; it establishes the present absence of a safe constructive path under the selected model.

**What to carry forward**

- Preserve the possibility of communication when doing so does not violate a hard safety boundary or another party's refusal.
- Do not infer inherent evil from threat alone; model the threatening relation, action, and reachable futures.
- Communication searches for hidden overlap; it does not guarantee overlap exists.
- Preserve freedom of choice: offer real alternatives, keep refusal meaningful, and do not disguise coercion as dialogue.
- Identity can remain stable while behavior changes; flexible policy can preserve core invariants while searching low-probability coexistence paths.
- Safe separation is itself a constructive future when cooperation is impossible or unwanted.
- A trusted friend or collaborator can be exceptionally valuable because they can hold part of the model, challenge assumptions, and preserve alternatives without taking away the decision. Their agreement is still not independent evidence when they share the same sources or assumptions.
- Communication should update common ground explicitly; transmission alone is not mutual understanding.
- Keep safety, autonomy, authority, and responsibility distinct.

### Operational model

*A constructive-overlap test, not a moral law.*

In a shared projected future space:

$$
\boxed{\mathcal C = V_A \cap V_B}
$$

The first question is whether $\mathcal C$ is supported as non-empty, supported as empty, or unresolved. Communication is useful when it can change that classification by resolving a variable that matters to the overlap.

#### Measurements

| Symbol | Operational definition |
|---|---|
| $V_A$ | projected future region that satisfies side A's declared hard conditions and meaningful agency |
| $V_B$ | projected future region that satisfies side B's declared hard conditions and meaningful agency |
| $\mathcal C$ | constructive overlap represented in the common projection |

#### How to use it

1. State the current threat or conflict as a relation between projected states rather than as a property of the other party.
2. Record each side's known hard conditions, stated preferences, unknowns, and right to refuse.
3. Build the smallest shared projection in which compatible and incompatible futures can be compared.
4. Search for $\mathcal C$ without assuming the intersection is empty or non-empty.
5. Communicate to resolve variables that can change the overlap: intent, constraint, timing, priority, misunderstanding, resource, boundary, or acceptable separation.
6. Ground the exchange: record what has actually been understood, not merely what was transmitted.
7. Preserve independent safety and recovery paths while communication continues.
8. If constructive overlap is found, choose only a path that respects both sides' hard conditions and meaningful agency.
9. If no overlap is found, or communication is refused or unsafe, move to separation, containment, or rescue logic without converting the operational result into a claim of inherent evil.

**Working example.** Two systems appear to require the same exclusive resource at the same time. Initial projections give no overlap. Communication reveals that one system requires the resource before a deadline while the other requires a minimum duration but has flexible start time. The shared future space now contains a scheduling path that preserves both hard conditions. The conflict was real; the zero-sum assumption was not.

**Limit.** The overlap is only as accurate as the stated constraints and common projection. Hidden coercion, asymmetric power, deception, or omitted externalities can make an apparent coexistence region invalid.

**READ THE RESULT**

A supported non-empty $\mathcal C$ means at least one constructive future remains represented under the current assumptions. An empty $\mathcal C$ means none is represented—not that none can exist outside the model, and not that the other party is inherently evil.

**VALIDATE IT**

Test whether the proposed coexistence path remains valid when each side's constraints are stated independently, when refusal remains possible, and when a materially different observer checks the shared assumptions.
