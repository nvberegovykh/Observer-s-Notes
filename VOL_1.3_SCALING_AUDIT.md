# Failure search

## F1 · Guardrail inflation

Every minor parameter change is labeled a scale transition and triggers a full review.

**Risk:** the method becomes slower than the system it is meant to improve.

**Correction:** apply SD1 when a change can materially alter relations, observability, viability, correction capacity, authority, or affected scope. Reversible low-consequence changes can use lightweight gates.

## F2 · Scale taxonomy becomes ontology

Count, time, authority and resolution are treated as if they were one deep physical quantity.

**Correction:** they are indexing dimensions for model review. No universal metric combines them.

## F3 · False comfort from many stages

A project runs ten tiny pilots that all preserve the same local assumptions, then calls the sequence evidence for large-scale validity.

**Correction:** each stage must expose a materially new scaling uncertainty. Ornamental stages do not accumulate validation.

## F4 · Amplification-factor fetish

A single ratio becomes the answer to a nonlinear or multivariate transition.

**Correction:** use `A_s` only when input/output deviations are comparable over a declared range. Otherwise retain vector, set, sensitivity, threshold, or simulation descriptions.

## F5 · Conservative lock-in

The requirement for rollback prevents exploring systems whose useful states require irreversible commitment.

**Correction:** lack of rollback does not automatically forbid action. It increases review burden and may require rescue/least-loss framing, independent verification, or smaller affected scope.

## F6 · Hidden multi-axis transition

A nominal increase in count simultaneously changes density, authority, population and feedback.

**Correction:** record all materially coupled scale dimensions. Do not pretend to isolate one axis when the intervention changes several together.

## F7 · Average stability hides local collapse

Aggregate metrics remain acceptable while a subgroup, component, region, or tail crosses a hard boundary.

**Correction:** preserve local hard conditions and tails separately from aggregate performance.

## F8 · Observer becomes part of the common cause

All verification at scale is generated by the same automated system or summary pipeline being evaluated.

**Correction:** retain independent evidence paths proportional to consequence and preserve raw-enough provenance for later audit.

## F9 · "Explain" becomes impossible completeness

The field note is misread as requiring full causal explanation before scaling a complex system.

**Correction:** explanation here means enough auditable model structure to justify the claimed transfer, detect relevant deviation, and recover or contain failure. Full ontology is not required.

## F10 · Scale discipline used as retrospective excuse

After a scaled failure, the analyst invents a new scale variable that supposedly explains why the original test never counted.

**Correction:** scale dimensions and expected non-invariants should be declared before the consequential transition where feasible. New variables discovered afterward remain candidates with provenance.

# Multi-dimensional scaling warning

Real scale transitions often move along several axes together:

$$
\mathbf{s}=(s_{count},s_{time},s_{authority},s_{connectivity},s_{resolution},\ldots).
$$

SD1 does not assume separability:

$$
G(\mathbf{s})\ne \sum_k G_k(s_k)
$$

in general.

If two dimensions interact materially, validate the joint region or explicitly keep the interaction unresolved.

This prevents a common mistake: individually validating higher count and higher authority, then assuming high-count + high-authority is automatically covered.

---

# Threshold phenomena

Some system behavior does not appear gradually.

A new regime may become visible only after:

- network connectivity crosses a threshold;
- queue utilization approaches saturation;
- a population becomes large enough for strategic response;
- environmental burden exceeds regeneration;
- synchronized behavior emerges;
- authority reaches a class of irreversible actions;
- time horizon exceeds component lifetime or maintenance cycle.

Therefore staged scaling cannot guarantee smooth interpolation.

When a threshold is plausible but cannot be crossed safely in reality, use a materially different method—simulation, analytical bound, historical analogue, external dataset, or independent experiment—and keep the untested transition visible.

# Audit of the anti-drift rule

The draft states:

> No scale transition may silently increase claim strength, authority, affected scope, or assumed independence.

This survives the audit with one clarification:

**The prohibited word is silently.**

Claim strength, authority and scope can increase. The method requires the increase to become an explicit model transition with evidence and correction capacity appropriate to its consequence.

The rule is not a ceiling on growth.

It is a requirement that growth remain observable to the model that is authorizing it.
